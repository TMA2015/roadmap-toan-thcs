## 2. §12 — Linear equations in two variables

Observed pedagogical/problem structures:

- geometric interpretation as a line;
- parameterized line families;
- finding a fixed point common to a family of lines;
- optimizing distance from the origin to a parameterized line;
- integer-coordinate points on a line;
- intercept/area constraints;
- reconstructing lines from coordinate conditions.

### CT09 implication

Useful as **Core-Support / Entrance10 bridge** for:

- demonstrating why parameter changes alter a line family.

Advanced optimization/integer-coordinate variants must not become Core merely because they appear here.

## 3. §13 — Systems of two linear equations

Observed progression extends well beyond routine solving:

### A. Parameterized systems

- solve after a specified parameter value;
- classify/biện luận a system according to a parameter;
- find parameter values for:
  - unique/no/infinitely many solutions;
  - integer solutions;
  - positive solutions;
  - constrained extrema involving the solution.

### B. Structural transformation

- systems whose equations require algebraic transformation before becoming linear;
- rational-form systems;
- absolute-value systems;
- systems where symmetry or substitution exposes a simpler structure.

### C. Integer / Diophantine flavor

- determine integer coefficients/solutions satisfying a system;
- use divisibility and parameterization to enumerate integer solutions.

### D. More than two unknowns

- three-variable linear systems;
- symmetric coefficient systems;
- relations among solutions rather than only raw elimination.

### Boundary conclusion

These structures are highly valuable for:
- **Entrance10** problem-type discovery;
- **Specialized-Challenge** extension;
- selecting canonical demonstrations of reasoning.

They are not evidence that all such structures belong in KNTT-Core.
